A fat separator does the same job as a gravy skimmer or a careful ladle, but with less mess and more speed. It lets you pour off clear broth or jus while leaving the fat behind, a simple tool that makes Sunday roasts, soups, and sauces noticeably better. When you’re shopping on a budget, the main trade-offs are capacity (smaller means you might need to work in batches), material (plastic is cheaper but can stain or warp), and how easily the stopper seals and cleans. The cheapest options often work fine for occasional use, but a well-designed budget separator should still have a tight stopper, a stable base, and a spout that pours without dripping. The two models here represent the best of the under-fifty-dollar range: one that’s the outright value king, and one that’s worth the extra investment if you cook frequently.
